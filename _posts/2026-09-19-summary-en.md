**Background**: The Intel Celeron 1200 uses the Tualatin core, which was part of the legacy Pentium III era based on the P6 microarchitecture. Unlike modern CPUs, older processors utilized physical substrate pins that physically engaged with the motherboard socket. The term 'substrate surgery' refers to the highly delicate process of soldering or splicing tiny wires and components directly onto the silicon's plastic backing material, which is typically the only option when a pin breaks at the root.
