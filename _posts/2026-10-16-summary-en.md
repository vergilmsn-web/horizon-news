## [Scientists detect the most distant Fast Radio Burst FRB 20240304B](https://www.solidot.org/story?sid=85619) ⭐️ 7.3/10

Scientists identified the most distant Fast Radio Burst, FRB 20240304B, using the MeerKAT radio telescope and the James Webb Space Telescope, locating its source in a galaxy from approximately 3 billion years after the Big Bang. The signal traveled for over 10 billion years, a distance more than double that of the previous FRB record. This breakthrough provides a rare glimpse into the young universe, demonstrating that the James Webb Space Telescope can identify host galaxies for extremely distant transients that are invisible to ground-based telescopes. The discovery strongly supports the magnetar origin theory for fast radio bursts by showing their occurrence in active, low-mass star-forming dwarf galaxies. The burst lasted only a few milliseconds, and the redshift of approximately 2.148 confirmed the host galaxy is a low-mass, metal-poor dwarf galaxy that is actively forming stars. The host galaxy was previously invisible to large ground-based telescopes due to being small and dim, but Webb's infrared capabilities allowed it to be accurately localized.

rss · Solidot · Oct 15, 07:11

**Background**: Fast Radio Bursts (FRBs) are enigmatic, millisecond-long pulses of radio energy of extragalactic origin that have baffled astronomers since their discovery. While magnetars (highly magnetic neutron stars) are a leading candidate for their source, it is not certain that they are responsible for all bursts. Ground-based telescopes are often limited in detecting these distant hosts due to the low brightness of the galaxies.
