## [Samsung Unveils First LPDDR5X-PIM with Embedded Logic at Hot Chips 2026](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/dram/hot-chips-2026-samsung-makes-lpddr5x-smart-with-logic-unit-in-memory-lpddr5x-pim-is-3-01x-faster-than-lpddr5x-in-ai-inference-with-8x-the-bandwidth) ⭐️ 8.5/10

Samsung detailed the industry's first LPDDR5X-PIM (Processing-in-Memory) at Hot Chips 2026, embedding logic units directly into DRAM to accelerate data-intensive workloads. The chip delivers 3.01x faster AI inference and 8x the bandwidth of standard LPDDR5X. This represents a significant milestone in memory architecture, as Samsung is commercializing processing-in-memory in a standard LPDDR5X form factor rather than as a niche research product. The technology could dramatically reduce the memory bandwidth bottleneck that limits AI inference performance on edge devices, smartphones, and servers. The LPDDR5X-PIM uses a JEDEC-standard 561-ball package with 16GB capacity across four dies per rank, and targets server, mobile, and client applications. This standardization means it can potentially drop into existing system designs without major architectural changes.

rss · Tom's Hardware · Aug 25, 18:31

**Background**: Processing-in-Memory (PIM), also called Compute-in-Memory (CIM), is a computer architecture concept where data operations are performed directly on memory rather than transferring data to CPU registers. This approach minimizes data movement between processors and memory, which has become the primary bottleneck for modern AI workloads. LPDDR5X is a low-power version of DDR5 memory commonly used in laptops, smartphones, and other power-constrained devices, offering high data transfer rates while consuming less energy than standard DDR5.

## [SK Hynix Next-Gen HBM4 Memory to Use Intel EMIB-T Packaging](https://www.techpowerup.com/351913/sk-hynix-next-gen-hbm-memory-to-use-intel-emib-t-packaging) ⭐️ 7.5/10

At Hot Chips 2026, SK Hynix revealed it is collaborating with Intel to integrate its next-generation HBM4 memory using Intel's EMIB and EMIB-T advanced packaging, alongside ongoing work with TSMC's CoWoS variants. HBM4 will double the pin count from approximately 1,024 to 2,048 pins and rely on 2.5D packaging, hybrid bonding, and 3D DRAM integration. This deal signals Intel Foundry's growing role in the AI chip packaging ecosystem and represents a significant step in diversifying advanced packaging supply away from TSMC's near-monopoly on CoWoS. For AI accelerator designers, having multiple qualified packaging options (EMIB-T alongside CoWoS-L/S/R) reduces supply-chain risk and may ease the persistent CoWoS capacity bottleneck. EMIB-T extends Intel's established EMIB technology by adding through-silicon vias (TSVs), which enable vertical power delivery across multiple stacked chips — a capability that aligns with SK Hynix's 3D memory module architecture. This vertical power delivery at the package level is a key enabler for stacking more HBM dies while maintaining signal and power integrity.

rss · TechPowerUp News · Aug 25, 08:23

**Background**: HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) is a stacked DRAM technology that provides the massive memory bandwidth required by AI accelerators such as GPUs and custom ASICs. Advanced packaging technologies like 2.5D and 3D integration are essential for connecting HBM stacks to logic dies at very high data rates. TSMC's CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate) has been the dominant 2.5D packaging solution for AI chips, available in CoWoS-S (silicon interposer), CoWoS-R (RDL-based), and CoWoS-L (with local silicon interconnects). Intel's EMIB (Embedded Multi-die Interconnect Bridge) is an alternative 2.5D approach using small embedded silicon bridges in an organic substrate, and EMIB-T adds TSVs for vertical power delivery. Through-silicon vias (TSVs) are vertical electrical connections passing completely through a silicon wafer or die, enabling 3D stacking with high density and low power consumption.
