## [Signal to Allow Registration Without Phone Number via Zero-Knowledge Proofs](https://community.signalusers.org/t/registration-without-a-phone-number/2222?page=10) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Signal is implementing zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) technology to enable users to register for the messaging service without providing a phone number, while still requiring a purchase through Google Play Billing to mitigate spam. The SMS verification option will remain available alongside this new ZKP-based registration method. This is a significant privacy milestone for one of the most widely used encrypted messaging apps, potentially allowing users to sign up without exposing their phone number — a common identifier that can link identities across services and leak through data breaches. It also opens Signal to users on devices without SIM cards, such as Android tablets, broadening accessibility. Per community discussion referencing Signal's commits, the ZKP registration flow will require a Google Play Billing purchase as an anti-spam measure, which has raised privacy concerns since it ties registration to a Google payment account. Additionally, the new release cycle reportedly allows Android tablets without a SIM to function as first-class Signal devices.

hackernews · Cider9986 · Sep 13, 21:47 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49689048)

**Background**: Signal is a privacy-focused, end-to-end encrypted messaging application that has traditionally required a phone number for registration, since it was originally designed as a replacement for SMS. Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) are a cryptographic method that allows one party to prove a statement is true without revealing the underlying data — for example, proving a user has the right to register without disclosing their phone number. This approach has been explored in authentication systems where one party wants to prove identity via secret information without the verifier learning that secret.

## [ASRock TempGuard Fails on $15,000 RTX PRO 6000 Due to Misplaced NTC Sensor](https://www.techpowerup.com/352658/asrocks-tempguard-failed-to-stop-a-psu-side-connector-meltdown-on-a-usd-15-000-rtx-pro-6000-rig) ⭐️ 6.5/10

A repair technician reports that a $15,000 NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Workstation Edition GPU paired with an ASRock Taichi TC-1650T PSU suffered a catastrophic PSU-side 16-pin connector meltdown, despite ASRock's TempGuard safety feature being active. The technician claims the NTC temperature sensor was incorrectly mounted on the ground line rather than the 12V line, rendering the protection system unable to detect rising temperatures. This incident reveals a potential design flaw in ASRock's safety-critical TempGuard system — if sensor placement errors affect multiple cables, the protection feature cannot be relied upon. For professionals and enthusiasts running $15,000 workstation-class GPUs, a non-functional thermal safeguard represents significant financial risk on top of an already well-documented connector melting problem. The GPU reportedly survived the incident, with damage confined to the PSU-side socket and cable, while the 12V-2x6 connector was fully seated at both ends. This is not the first melting incident for the ASRock Taichi TC-1650T and RTX PRO 6000 pairing — a similar failure was reported back in March — though TempGuard has previously been credited with saving a shunt-modded RTX 5090 pulling over 1,300W from melting.

rss · TechPowerUp News · Sep 13, 18:12

**Background**: The 12V-2x6 connector (also known as 12VHPWR) is a 16-pin standard introduced by NVIDIA in 2022 to deliver up to 600W (and later 660W) to high-end GPUs, replacing older 6- and 8-pin power connectors. Since its debut, the connector has been plagued by widespread melting incidents driven by high current draw and imperfect contact geometry, prompting PSU manufacturers to add supplementary thermal safeguards. ASRock's TempGuard embeds an NTC thermistor — a temperature-sensitive resistor whose resistance decreases as temperature rises — into the 12V-2x6 cable so the PSU can shut down before the connector reaches dangerous temperatures. Because NTC sensors must be physically placed on the conductor carrying the high current to accurately measure heat, mounting it on the ground line instead of the 12V line would defeat the entire purpose of the system.
