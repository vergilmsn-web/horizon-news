## [Google-Marvell Deal Expands Custom Silicon Beyond TPUs](https://www.eetimes.com/googles-marvell-deal-shows-custom-silicon-spreading-beyond-the-tpu/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Google has expanded its partnership with Marvell Technology, signaling that custom silicon specialization is now extending beyond AI accelerators like TPUs into memory, networking, storage, and data movement within data centers. This move reflects a broader architectural shift in hyperscaler infrastructure, where specialized chips are being deployed across the entire data center stack rather than just AI compute. It has significant implications for traditional chip vendors like Intel and NVIDIA, and underscores how hyperscalers are increasingly taking control of their silicon roadmaps to optimize performance, power efficiency, and cost at scale. Marvell already possesses a strong portfolio in interconnects, Ethernet switches (Teralynx for data centers), network adapters (FastLinQ), and security offload, making it a natural partner for Google to extend custom silicon beyond AI compute. Unlike general-purpose processors, custom ASICs deliver better performance and lower energy consumption for specific workloads, which is the core rationale behind this trend.

**Background**: Google's Tensor Processing Unit (TPU) is an application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) developed internally since 2015 to accelerate neural network and machine learning workloads, supporting frameworks like TensorFlow, JAX, and PyTorch. Hyperscalers such as Google, Amazon, and Microsoft have increasingly designed custom silicon to gain advantages in performance-per-watt and total cost of ownership for AI training and inference. Marvell Technology is a semiconductor company specializing in custom silicon design and a broad portfolio of interconnect and networking products, and has been emerging as a major partner for hyperscaler custom-AI-chip programs.

## [California unanimously passes Linux exemption from age-verification law](https://www.tomshardware.com/software/linux/california-lawmakers-unanimously-pass-linux-exemption-from-age-verification-law-software-distributed-under-the-gpl-mit-bsd-and-apache-licenses-are-exempt) ⭐️ 7.5/10

California's legislature unanimously passed Assembly Bill 1856, exempting open-source operating systems and software distributed under the GPL, MIT, BSD, and Apache licenses from the state's Digital Age Assurance Act (DAAA). The exemption comes months before the age-verification law is set to take effect on January 1, 2027. This is a significant win for the open-source community, as the DAAA would have placed an enormous compliance burden on Linux distributors and open-source projects that ship operating systems or system-level software, potentially requiring them to collect age information at device setup and transmit age-bracket signals to application developers. The exemption protects some of the most widely-used open-source licenses from regulations designed for commercial platform providers. AB 1856 specifically targets the four most common open-source license families: GPL (copyleft), MIT (permissive), BSD (permissive), and Apache (permissive with patent grant). Under the original DAAA, developers receiving an age signal are deemed to have actual knowledge of a user's age range across all platforms, creating significant liability for downstream application developers.

rss · Tom's Hardware · Aug 29, 15:57

**Background**: The Digital Age Assurance Act (DAAA), originally AB 1043, was enacted to require operating system providers to collect age information from users at device account setup and transmit age-bracket signals (under 13, 13–16, 16–18, or 18+) to application developers, who would then use this information to restrict minors' access to certain content. The law targets commercial platform gatekeepers like Apple, Google, and Microsoft. Open-source software is developed collaboratively and distributed publicly without a commercial vendor controlling the user relationship, making age-verification compliance practically impossible for community projects. Open-source licenses fall into two broad categories: copyleft licenses like GPL require derivative works to adopt the same license, while permissive licenses like MIT, BSD, and Apache impose minimal restrictions on reuse.

## [Samsung Showcases Processing-in-Memory Tech at Hot Chips 2026](https://chipsandcheese.com/p/hot-chips-2026-samsungs-processing) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Samsung presented its latest Processing-in-Memory (PIM) technology at Hot Chips 2026, which performs computation directly within memory to alleviate the memory bandwidth bottleneck that limits AI and other compute-intensive workloads. The company continues to develop this architecture following earlier presentations in 2020 and 2021, with the goal of reducing costly data movement between memory and processors. This matters because the memory wall has become one of the primary constraints on AI scaling, as model sizes balloon and data movement energy dominates total system power. If PIM can deliver practical gains, it could reshape how AI accelerators and high-performance computing systems are built, potentially benefiting major semiconductor players willing to invest in non-traditional architectures. PIM embeds simple compute units directly into DRAM to bypass off-chip bandwidth limits imposed by PCB traces, package pins, and thermal caps. Samsung's Aquabolt-XL is noted as the first commercially fabricated PIM device. However, commenters note that PIM requires careful data placement and is best suited to regular, structured workloads like matrix multiplication in AI inference, gaming, and cryptography.

hackernews · ingve · Aug 29, 06:06 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49487341)

**Background**: Processing-in-Memory, also known as Compute-in-Memory (CIM), is a computer architecture concept where data operations are performed directly on the data stored in memory, eliminating the need to shuttle data to CPU registers. The idea dates back at least to the early 1980s, as discussed in the Conway & Mead VLSI design textbook. The 'memory wall' refers to the growing disparity between processor speed and memory access speed, which has become especially acute for large AI models that require enormous amounts of data movement. Hot Chips is a prestigious annual symposium on high-performance microprocessors held at Stanford since 1989.

## [vphone-cli: Boot a Virtual iPhone via Apple's Virtualization.framework](https://github.com/Lakr233/vphone-cli) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The open-source project vphone-cli enables booting a virtual iPhone (iOS 26) on a Mac by leveraging Apple's Virtualization.framework together with iOS kernel images from Apple's PCC/cloudOS research infrastructure, pairing them with the iOS user-space and applying patches to make the system runnable. A companion vphone-mcp tool exposes the virtual device to AI agents via the Model Context Protocol, allowing agents to take screenshots, navigate the UI, and automate app testing. This gives iOS developers and security researchers a free, local way to run a near-real iOS instance for testing—something previously limited to paid services like Corellium. The built-in MCP integration is also notable as one of the first concrete examples of using AI agents to drive a full mobile OS for automated UI testing. Unlike emulation (e.g., Corellium), this project uses Apple's own Virtualization.framework on a real Mac CPU, so apps can still detect it as a non-real device. During iOS setup, users must avoid selecting Japan or the EU as their region because the VM cannot satisfy additional regulatory checks required for those markets.

hackernews · hentrep · Aug 28, 23:02 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49485267)

**Background**: Apple's Virtualization.framework is a set of high-level APIs that let Macs run virtual machines natively on Apple silicon or Intel hardware, and is the same framework that underpins tools like the macOS Sonoma Virtualization framework and UTM-like workflows. Corellium is a commercial product that emulates iOS devices on non-Apple hardware for security research, which differs from running real iOS kernel binaries under virtualization. The Model Context Protocol (MCP) is an open standard, popularized by Anthropic, that lets AI assistants connect to external tools and data sources so they can perform actions like taking screenshots or invoking APIs.

## [DLSS 5 Patched to Run on RTX 4000 Ada GPUs via Modding](https://www.techpowerup.com/352086/dlss-5-patched-to-work-on-rtx-4000-ada-lovelace-gpus-despite-no-official-support) ⭐️ 5.5/10

Modders on the RenoDX Discord, led by a user called 'Uncle Burrito,' successfully patched NVIDIA's leaked DLSS 5 DLL to run on RTX 4000-series Ada Lovelace GPUs by swapping the Blackwell-only sm_120 CUDA binaries with Ada-compatible ones, with Tom's Hardware confirming the fix on an RTX 4080. This demonstrates that NVIDIA's upcoming DLSS 5 neural rendering technology is not inherently locked to Blackwell hardware, giving RTX 4000 owners hope for official or unofficial support and highlighting the modding community's ability to reverse-engineer vendor restrictions before any official announcement. The leaked DLSS 5 DLL was originally compiled only for Blackwell's sm_120 architecture and originated from an NBA 2K27 early-access build; however, the underlying AI model uses FP8 precision, which Ada Lovelace natively supports, meaning only the compiled CUDA binaries needed replacement for the patch to function.

rss · TechPowerUp News · Aug 28, 17:22

**Background**: DLSS (Deep Learning Super Sampling) is NVIDIA's AI-driven upscaling and frame-generation technology. DLSS 5, also referred to as DLSS Neural Rendering, goes further by using AI to replace traditional lighting and materials, processing a full 4K frame in under 16 milliseconds. Ada Lovelace is the architecture behind RTX 4000-series cards, while Blackwell powers the RTX 5000 series. The DLL was leaked via the NBA 2K27 early-access build, and modders used ReShade (a generic post-processing injector by crosire) together with RenoDX (the 'Renovation Engine for DirectX Games') to inject the patched DLL into other games.

## [Microsoft-backed AI data center faces community backlash over unpermitted turbines](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/data-centers/microsoft-backed-ai-data-center-faces-multiple-complaints-from-community-issues-range-from-unpermitted-gas-turbines-to-illegal-construction-and-noise-pollution) ⭐️ 5.5/10

The Microsoft-linked $19.4B DataOne data center in Vineland, New Jersey, is facing multiple community complaints ranging from unpermitted gas turbines and a reported 1.5-million-gallon LNG tank to allegations of illegal construction, noise pollution, and potential water supply issues. This case exemplifies the growing friction between hyperscale AI infrastructure expansion and local communities, particularly as operators increasingly deploy behind-the-meter gas turbines to bypass grid bottlenecks. The outcome could set precedents for how AI-era data centers balance speed-to-power against environmental compliance and local acceptance. On-site (behind-the-meter) gas turbines have become a popular workaround for data centers facing utility interconnection delays, but they introduce complex regulatory exposure around emissions, fuel supply, and permitting. Roughly 56% of U.S. data center electricity still comes from fossil fuels, and modern AI accelerators such as NVIDIA H100 consume 400–700 watts each, dramatically increasing power density and fueling this trend toward co-located generation.

rss · Tom's Hardware · Aug 29, 11:00

**Background**: A hyperscale data center houses tens of thousands of GPUs, networking gear, and massive cooling systems; AI training and inference workloads are far more power- and heat-intensive than traditional cloud workloads, which is why developers are increasingly building 'AI supercomputers' rather than conventional facilities. When the public grid cannot deliver power fast enough, operators install on-site gas turbines (so-called behind-the-meter generation) to come online sooner, but these installations still require air, noise, and construction permits from local authorities. New Jersey has become a key hub for such projects along the Northeast corridor, where land, water, and grid capacity are all constrained.
