The Global Electronics Association issued a new industry alert warning that PCB lead times have extended to 16-20 weeks due to AI server demand. North American PCB bookings have surged by 56.1%, vastly outpacing a 13.3% increase in actual shipments. This supply chain bottleneck threatens to impact non-tech sectors like automotive, medical, and defense industries. The shortage is expected to last through 2027, potentially causing significant production delays and price hikes across the global economy. AI server boards require 40 to 60 layers compared to standard 12-layer boards, drastically increasing the demand for raw materials like glass fabric and copper foil. Motherboard prices have already increased by 15% to 50% due to these rising PCB costs.

rss · TechPowerUp News · Nov 20, 20:19

**Background**: Printed Circuit Boards (PCBs) are the physical base of electronic circuits. The 2021 chip crisis was a massive global shortage of semiconductors caused by post-pandemic demand and supply chain shifts. This new bottleneck occurs because the raw materials required for high-layer boards are physically scarce and cannot be scaled up quickly to meet the massive infrastructure demands of AI data centers.

## [FBI Sting Exposes Ransomware Decryption Service as Ransom Paying Fraud](https://www.solidot.org/story?sid=85950) ⭐️ 7.3/10

The FBI revealed through a sting operation that RansomwareHelp.com, which claims to decrypt data for free using advanced algorithms, is actually paying ransoms to attackers and profiting from the markup. The company's co-founder was found to have no technical decryption capability, instead acting as a middleman who charged victims significantly more than the actual ransom cost. This case exposes a significant fraud in the cybersecurity industry where victims are exploited by companies that misrepresent their technical capabilities. It highlights the critical need for security practitioners to verify the credentials of third-party decryption services before relying on them during a crisis. The company had been operating for six years, claiming 28 years of industry experience and a team of 80 experts, but its CTO, Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, was caught in a Rhode Island lawsuit filing. The FBI agents played dual roles as both victims and attackers to expose the fraud.

rss · Solidot · Nov 21, 10:11

**Background**: Ransomware is a type of malware that encrypts a victim's files, demanding a payment for the decryption key. Many victims, facing severe operational or data loss, turn to third-party security firms for recovery services. In this case, the FBI used a 'sting' operation where an agent pretended to be a victim and an attacker to trap the company in a lie.
