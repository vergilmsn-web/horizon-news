## [Buckmaster and Alpoge Prove Finite-Time Blow-up for Unforced Navier-Stokes](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Mathematicians Tristan Buckmaster and Alpoge have proven that the unforced Navier-Stokes equations can exhibit finite-time blow-up, meaning that solutions can become infinite in a finite amount of time. This breakthrough builds upon recent findings that demonstrated a similar phenomenon for the forced version of the equations. This result directly addresses a major open problem in fluid dynamics and mathematics by showing that the Navier-Stokes equations can break down under specific conditions, even without external forces. It significantly advances the understanding of turbulence and the limits of classical fluid mechanics, potentially resolving a component of a Millennium Prize Problem. The proof specifically applies to the unforced version of the equations, which is considered more physically realistic than the forced variant previously identified by OpenAI. This distinction is crucial because it implies the instability is intrinsic to the mathematical structure of the equations themselves rather than an artifact of specific external boundary conditions.

hackernews · trotro · Nov 20, 15:34 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50576232)

**Background**: The Navier-Stokes equations are a set of partial differential equations that describe the motion of viscous fluid substances, and the question of whether their solutions remain smooth or can blow up in finite time is a famous unsolved problem. A 'blow-up' refers to a situation where the mathematical solution ceases to exist or becomes infinite after a finite amount of time, which would indicate a breakdown in the model's predictive power.

**Discussion**: The community is astonished that this result and the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis were both achieved on the same day, marking a historic moment for mathematics. JEDEC has officially released the initial specifications for DDR6 memory, delivering 2.35x more bandwidth than DDR5 and shrinking DIMM size by 44%.

## [Windows 11 Enables RDMA over USB4 for Distributed Workloads](https://www.techpowerup.com/355062/microsoft-brings-rdma-over-usb4-to-windows-11-for-daisy-chained-pcs) ⭐️ 7.5/10

Microsoft has enabled Remote Direct Memory Access (RDMA) over the USB4 protocol on Windows 11, allowing multiple PCs to be daisy-chained for distributed processing. This new feature reduces data access latency significantly compared to standard Ethernet-over-USB4 configurations. This development enables home and prosumer environments to pool hardware resources for latency-sensitive tasks like AI inference, breaking the limitations of single-device compute. It provides a practical, low-latency alternative for distributed computing without requiring specialized high-speed server hardware. RDMA over USB4 achieves round-trip latencies of 28 to 50 microseconds, which is roughly four times faster than the 116 to 194 microseconds observed with Ethernet over USB4 using UDP. The USB4 protocol supports up to 80 Gbps bandwidth, facilitating high-speed data transfer between interconnected PCs.

rss · TechPowerUp News · Nov 20, 17:51

**Background**: Remote Direct Memory Access (RDMA) is a networking technology that allows one computer's processor to read data directly from another computer's memory without involving the intermediate CPUs or operating systems, reducing overhead. USB4 is a universal high-speed interface that supports bandwidths up to 80 Gbps, traditionally used for peripherals but now being leveraged for inter-node communication. Distributed computing involves splitting large tasks across multiple connected machines to process them faster than a single unit could.
