Salience Labs raised $125 million to accelerate optical circuit switching, marking a major investment push for silicon photonics. This funding is part of a broader industry race to deploy silicon-based photonic interconnects and beat the power limits of copper. Scaling beyond copper interconnects is critical for future data centers and AI hardware due to power and bandwidth constraints. This $125M investment validates optical circuit switching as a core enabler for high-performance computing infrastructure. Salience Labs is specifically advancing optical circuit switching, a technology that routes light directly to avoid O-E-O conversion latency and power consumption. The silicon components typically operate at the 1.55 micrometre wavelength standard in fiber optics.

rss · EE Times · Nov 4, 19:51

**Background**: Traditional computer chips use copper wires to transfer data, which becomes highly inefficient and power-intensive at high speeds. Silicon photonics moves data using photons in micro-etched waveguides on a silicon-on-insulator (SOI) wafer instead of electrons. Optical circuit switching further improves this by eliminating the need for repeated electrical conversions, creating direct, high-bandwidth optical paths between network nodes.
