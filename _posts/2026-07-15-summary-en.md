## [2026 Fields Medal recipients appear leaked from ICM website](https://www.solidot.org/story?sid=84823) ⭐️ 8.3/10

Hidden fields labeled 'Fields Medal Lecture' were discovered in the frontend code of the ICM 2026 website's schedule page, naming Yu Deng, John Pardon, Jacob Tsimerman, and Hong Wang as likely recipients. The 2026 International Congress of Mathematicians is scheduled for July 23, and the Fields Medal — unlike the Nobel Prize — notifies winners in advance, making leaks possible. The Fields Medal is the most prestigious award in mathematics, awarded only every four years to researchers under 40. If confirmed, this would mark the first time two mathematicians of Chinese origin receive the medal in the same cycle — a milestone for Chinese mathematics beyond Shing-Tung Yau's 1982 win — and signals recognition of landmark results including a 'once-in-a-century' proof of the 3D Kakeya conjecture. The cited contributions are: Deng on Hilbert's sixth problem (axiomatization of physics), Wang on the 3D Kakeya set conjecture (the Wang–Zahl proof), Pardon on Gromov's knot theory problem, and Tsimerman on the André–Oort conjecture. The leak is unconfirmed and precedent exists (similar leaks from frontend code have occurred before), so official confirmation from the IMU is still pending.

rss · Solidot · Jul 14, 06:19

**Background**: The Fields Medal, established in 1936, is awarded at each International Congress of Mathematicians (ICM) to up to four mathematicians under the age of 40 — a deliberate age limit designed to recognize outstanding early-career work. The Kakeya conjecture asks how small a set in Euclidean space can be while containing a unit line segment in every direction; its 3D version was an open problem for roughly 50 years until the Wang–Zahl proof. Hilbert's sixth problem, posed in 1900, calls for the axiomatization of physics — notably probability and continuum mechanics. The André–Oort conjecture concerns special points on Shimura varieties and was fully proven in 2021. Unlike the Nobel Prize, the Fields Medal committee informs winners privately before the ceremony, which has historically led to premature disclosures.

## [AI Coding Tools Don't Solve Software's Coordination Problem](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/7/13/the-tower-keeps-rising/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Developer Armin Ronacher published an essay arguing that AI-assisted programming dramatically boosts individual code production but fails to address the fundamental coordination, architectural, and shared understanding challenges that have always constrained large-scale software development. The piece draws explicit parallels to the 'Lisp Curse' concept, suggesting that the Lisp community's ability to build things easily never translated into a rich ecosystem of collaboratively maintained software. This argument challenges the prevailing narrative in the tech industry that AI coding assistants will automatically lead to more ambitious and successful software projects. For engineering leaders, architects, and organizations investing heavily in AI tooling, the essay raises important questions about whether productivity gains at the individual level translate into systemic improvements in software quality and maintainability at scale. The essay emphasizes that the 'shared language' of a software project—encompassing concepts, boundaries, invariants, ownership, and architectural rationale—is rarely documented in one place but distributed across code reviews, conversations, and accumulated experience. AI agents currently lack the ability to internalize and propagate this tacit knowledge, which means faster code generation may actually amplify architectural debt if applied without strong engineering judgment.

hackernews · cdrnsf · Jul 14, 16:57 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48909785)

**Background**: The 'Lisp Curse' is a concept discussed in the Lisp community, notably by Richard P. Gabriel, describing how the extreme flexibility and power of Lisp made it easy for individual programmers to solve their own problems but discouraged the collaborative effort needed to build robust, general-purpose libraries and frameworks. This is related to Gabriel's famous 'Worse is Better' thesis about how simpler, less elegant designs often achieve greater adoption. AI-assisted software development refers to the use of large language models and AI agents to help write, review, and modify code, a practice that has rapidly gained adoption since 2023 with tools like GitHub Copilot, Cursor, and terminal-based coding agents.

## [Intel Shifts Most Nova Lake Production In-House as 18A Yields Improve](https://www.techpowerup.com/350751/intel-shifts-majority-of-nova-lake-production-in-house-amid-yield-improvements) ⭐️ 7.5/10

According to a KeyBanc Capital Markets research note, Intel has reversed its earlier plan to dual-source Nova Lake compute tiles and will now manufacture 80–90% of them in-house on Intel Foundry's 18A node, with only a small portion going to TSMC's N2 process. The shift is driven by significantly improved 18A yields, which have reached defect-density levels around D0=0.1, making high-volume production economically viable. This is a significant vote of confidence in Intel Foundry's flagship 18A process and a major strategic signal that Intel is increasingly willing to rely on its own manufacturing for flagship consumer products. It also affects TSMC's expected N2 revenue and reshapes competitive dynamics in the leading-edge foundry market, potentially influencing how external customers evaluate Intel Foundry. The 18A node's defect rate is reported at D0=0.1 to D0=0.2, with Intel likely achieving the lower end thanks to months of high-volume learning from Panther Lake production, which runs at around 30,000 wafers per month. Compute tiles are chiplet-based building blocks, allowing a single SoC to mix tiles manufactured on different process nodes.

rss · TechPowerUp News · Jul 14, 13:33

**Background**: Nova Lake-S is Intel's upcoming next-generation desktop processor family, expected to succeed Panther Lake. Intel 18A is the company's most advanced production node—named for '1.8 Angstroms'—and is an Angstrom-class process featuring gate-all-around (GAA) RibbonFET transistors and PowerVia backside power delivery. TSMC's competing N2 is also a 2 nm-class GAA nanosheet process, and is TSMC's first node to adopt the GAA architecture. Chiplet or 'tile'-based designs break a processor into multiple smaller dies (e.g., compute, I/O, GPU) that are integrated into a single package, letting manufacturers mix and match nodes for different functions to optimize cost and yield.

## [Programmable Thermal Material Steers Heat Without Continuous Power](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/manufacturing/researchers-create-programmable-material-that-can-steer-heat-and-remember-its-state-without-power-breakthrough-could-eventually-aid-ai-chip-cooling-and-silicon-photonics) ⭐️ 7.5/10

Researchers have developed a programmable material that can actively control and redirect heat flow while retaining its configuration without continuous power input. The breakthrough combines a magneto-optical material with a phase-change material known as GST (germanium-antimony-tellurium). As AI chips grow more powerful and densely packed, thermal management has become a critical bottleneck limiting performance and reliability. A material that can programmatically steer heat and remember its state opens new possibilities for passive thermal regulation in AI hardware, silicon photonics, and infrared devices. The material works by combining magneto-optical properties with phase-change functionality from GST, allowing it to switch modes and direct thermal radiation like data. The non-volatile state retention means it can maintain thermal routing settings without ongoing power consumption, and the design breaks thermal reciprocity — a fundamental principle in heat transfer.

rss · Tom's Hardware · Jul 14, 09:30

**Background**: Thermal management is increasingly important in modern computing: as transistors shrink and power densities rise, chips generate more heat in less space, limiting clock speeds and reliability. Silicon photonics is an emerging technology that uses light instead of electrical signals to transmit data on chips, enabling faster communication and lower power consumption. Phase-change materials like GST are well-known for their use in rewritable optical discs and emerging memory technologies, as they can switch between amorphous and crystalline states with different optical and electrical properties. Magneto-optical materials interact with light in ways that can be controlled by magnetic fields, enabling manipulation of thermal radiation.
