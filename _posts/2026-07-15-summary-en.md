## [ASML's High NA EUV Enters High-Volume Manufacturing with Intel 18A](https://www.techpowerup.com/350772/asml-high-na-euv-technology-enters-high-volume-manufacturing-with-intel-18a) ⭐️ 8.5/10

ASML announced that Intel Foundry is using its High NA EUV lithography system on the Intel 18A process node for the first time in high-volume manufacturing, specifically to produce layers of the Intel Core Ultra Series 3 processors (codenamed Panther Lake). This marks the debut of High NA EUV in a production environment rather than just research or pilot lines. High NA EUV is widely regarded as essential for extending Moore's Law into the sub-2nm era, and its transition from development to high-volume manufacturing removes a major uncertainty in the industry's roadmap. The milestone validates the technology's economics and reliability, paving the way for broader adoption by other leading-edge fabs such as TSMC and Samsung in subsequent nodes. The High NA EUV platform, known as EXE, increases the numerical aperture from 0.33 (standard EUV) to 0.55, enabling a critical dimension (CD) of approximately 8nm and more precise patterning for advanced nodes. Intel 18A is an Angstrom-class node (~1.8Å) that also introduces RibbonFET gate-all-around transistors and PowerVia backside power delivery — making it a fitting showcase for the new lithography tool.

rss · TechPowerUp News · Jul 15, 07:09

**Background**: Photolithography is the process of using light to print circuit patterns onto silicon wafers, and its resolution is governed by the wavelength of light and the numerical aperture (NA) of the projection optics. Standard EUV lithography, which entered production around 2018, uses light with a 13.5nm wavelength and a 0.33 NA lens system. High NA EUV represents the next evolutionary step, with a larger 0.55 NA anamorphic lens designed by ZEISS SMT to print finer features required at the most advanced process nodes. Intel 18A is part of Intel's foundry strategy to reclaim process leadership, combining next-generation transistor architectures with new lithography capabilities.
