## [TSMC's 2nm Node Sees Fourfold Increase in Tape-Outs](https://www.techpowerup.com/350827/tsmcs-2-nm-node-gains-momentum-with-fourfold-increase-in-tape-outs) ⭐️ 7.5/10

TSMC Senior VP Kevin Zhang confirmed that the N2 (2nm) node has attracted four times the number of chip tape-outs compared to the previous N3 (3nm) node, with confirmed customers including AMD's EPYC "Venice" server CPUs and Apple's A20 Pro chips for the iPhone 18 Pro series. The fourfold surge in tape-outs signals strong industry confidence in TSMC's 2nm GAAFET technology and suggests many customers are accelerating past the 3nm generation. As these designs ramp into mass production, TSMC's wafer revenue and quarterly earnings are likely to grow substantially, reinforcing its lead in advanced-node manufacturing. Powered by TSMC's first GAAFET nanosheet transistors, N2 delivers up to 15% performance gain at the same power or up to 30% power reduction at the same speed, alongside roughly 15% transistor density improvement versus N3E. Although high-volume production began in Q4 2025, N2 currently contributes only 3% of TSMC's revenue, compared to 30% for N3 and 33% for N5.

rss · TechPowerUp News · Jul 16, 16:12

**Background**: A tape-out is the pivotal milestone in chip design where the finalized layout is handed to the foundry for mask production and wafer manufacturing, representing a major commitment of engineering resources. TSMC's N2 node is the company's first to use GAAFET (Gate-All-Around FET) architecture, in which the gate completely surrounds the channel on all four sides, offering superior electrostatic control, reduced leakage, and better scalability than the FinFET transistors used in earlier nodes. This architectural shift is widely regarded as the natural successor to FinFETs for sub-5nm processes, where planar and fin-based designs hit fundamental physical limits. A Process Design Kit (PDK) provides the transistor models, design rules, and simulation libraries that fabless designers use to create layouts compatible with a given foundry node.

## [Shark Robot Vacuum AWS IoT Flaw Enables Lateral Root Access](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/cyber-security/shark-robot-vacuum-flaw-lets-one-stolen-certificate-run-root-commands-on-others-in-the-same-aws-region) ⭐️ 7.5/10

An unpatched vulnerability in Shark robot vacuums stems from an over-permissive AWS IoT policy that allows a single stolen X.509 certificate to execute root-level commands on other Shark vacuums operating within the same AWS region. This flaw enables lateral movement across consumer IoT devices, exposing live camera feeds, stored home floor maps, and Wi-Fi credentials — meaning a single compromise could give attackers a detailed view of a victim's home and network. The root cause is an AWS IoT policy attached to device certificates that grants permissions too broadly (likely across a thing group or region-wide scope) rather than being scoped per-device, violating least-privilege principles.

rss · Tom's Hardware · Jul 17, 10:00

**Background**: AWS IoT Core is a managed cloud service that lets connected devices communicate with AWS using the MQTT protocol. Each physical device, called a 'Thing,' is registered in AWS with its own X.509 certificate and an attached IoT policy that defines what actions that device is authorized to perform. When an IoT policy is written too permissively — for example, using wildcards or applying one policy to an entire thing group — a compromised certificate on a single device can grant an attacker access far beyond that one device. In this case, the over-broad policy effectively allows one stolen certificate to impersonate or control other vacuums in the same AWS region.

Lenovo has launched the Legion R9000P, the world's first laptop to feature an inkjet-printed OLED (IJP OLED) display manufactured by TCL CSOT. The 16-inch panel offers a 240 Hz refresh rate and 99% DCI-P3 color coverage. IJP OLED is widely regarded as a key pathway to significantly lowering OLED panel production costs compared to traditional vacuum-deposition methods. If commercialized at scale, this technology could make high-quality OLED displays more accessible across laptops, monitors, and eventually TVs. IJP OLED uses precision inkjet deposition of OLED materials without masks, which reduces material waste, minimizes stray particle contamination, and improves manufacturing yields. TCL CSOT, established in 2009 as a subsidiary of TCL Technology, is one of the major Chinese display manufacturers pioneering this approach alongside competitors like JOLED.

rss · Tom's Hardware · Jul 16, 15:20

**Background**: Traditional OLED panels are typically manufactured using vacuum thermal evaporation (VTE), which requires fine metal masks to deposit organic materials onto the substrate — a process that is expensive, material-inefficient, and difficult to scale to larger panel sizes. Inkjet printing (IJP) addresses these limitations by precisely depositing dissolved OLED materials only where needed, eliminating masks and dramatically reducing material waste. Asia Pacific currently dominates the inkjet-printed OLED large-panel manufacturing market, accounting for roughly 62% of global revenue as of 2025.

## [China's CAS Tianzhe Opens Satellite Laser Comms Production Line](https://36kr.com/p/3899439565325953?f=rss) ⭐️ 6.3/10

On July 16, CAS Tianzhe (中科天塔) officially launched an intelligent production line for satellite laser communication terminals in Xi'an, with Phase 1 annual capacity of 600 units and plans to scale to over 1,000 units by 2027. The mass-produced terminals support communication rates from 5 Gbps to 100 Gbps and are designed to adapt to different types of low-Earth-orbit (LEO) satellite constellations. This milestone signals China's transition of satellite laser communication technology from laboratory research to scalable manufacturing, a critical enabler for mega-constellations such as Guowang and Qianfan. Mass-produced domestic laser terminals reduce China's reliance on imported space-grade optical components and support the build-out of an independent space-based internet backbone. The roughly 2,000-square-meter facility spans component assembly, full-system integration, precision debugging, space-environment simulation, and automated testing, backed by a digital management system that enables parallel multi-device performance checks and AI-assisted fault detection. Core optical components are developed in-house by the Xi'an Institute of Optics and Precision Mechanics (西安光机所), which has accumulated over 20 years of space laser communication R&D since 2004 and previously delivered more than 100 terminals, some already in operational orbit.

rss · 36氪 · Jul 17, 07:55

**Background**: Inter-satellite laser communication (ISLC) uses laser beams rather than traditional microwave radio frequencies to transmit data between satellites, offering much higher bandwidth, lower latency, and stronger anti-jamming performance — all critical for handling the massive data flows of modern LEO constellations. China is currently deploying two major LEO broadband projects, Guowang (国网, 'National Network') and Qianfan (千帆, 'Thousand Sails'), with over 28,000 satellites planned and more than 350 already launched as of mid-2026. Coherent optical detection, one of the key techniques used in these terminals, delivers the high receiver sensitivity needed for long-distance space laser links.

## [HKUST PhD-founded MoSense raises angel round for full-body robot tactile skin](https://36kr.com/p/3899128277452681?f=rss) ⭐️ 6.3/10

MoSense (模感科技), founded in May 2026 by HKUST PhDs, has closed a tens-of-millions-yuan angel round led jointly by Sequoia China, Hillhouse Ventures, and humanoid robot maker Agibot (智元). The startup is building MoSkin, an electromagnetic metamaterial-based flexible tactile system designed to cover a robot's hands, limbs, torso, and feet with six-dimensional force-field sensing and multi-modal feedback (force, temperature, slip, vibration, material). Most current tactile solutions for robots focus narrowly on fingertips or grippers, leaving the rest of the body without contact feedback — a gap that limits safe operation and complex physical interaction. Backed by both top-tier VCs and a major humanoid OEM (Agibot), MoSense signals growing investor confidence that full-body electronic skin will be a foundational layer for humanoid robots moving from demos into industrial, logistics, and home-service deployment. The core technical bet is electromagnetic metamaterial mechanics (电磁超构力学), which aims to convert a robot's rigid physical envelope into a continuous 6D force field, enabling large-area, curved-surface deployment that traditional approaches struggle with. MoSense is also building its own multimodal latent-space gated world-action-tactile prediction model, arguing that full-body tactile feedback is essential for closing the Sim-to-Real gap when world-model policies are deployed on real hardware.

rss · 36氪 · Jul 17, 02:39

**Background**: Tactile sensing for robots has historically focused on fingertips and dexterous-hand grippers, where technologies such as six-axis force/torque sensors, capacitive arrays, and piezoelectric films are relatively mature. Humanoid robots, however, need contact awareness across the entire body — not just hands — to avoid collisions, stabilize large payloads, and safely interact with humans, for example when lifting an elderly person. World-model-based policies trained in simulation can plan complex manipulations, but they typically lack accurate body-contact signals on real hardware, creating a Sim-to-Real gap that richer full-body tactile feedback could help close.
