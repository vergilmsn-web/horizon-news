Ledger confirmed that hardware wallets distributed through the Southeast Asian retailer CryptoBilis were physically modified by attackers to include a hidden spy chip that steals cryptographic keys. Research from Tibane Labs revealed the devices had modified batteries and embedded ST STM32G431KBU6 microcontrollers paired with cellular modules to exfiltrate data. This high-impact supply chain attack proves that even secure offline devices like hardware wallets can be compromised before they reach users. It underscores severe financial risks for cryptocurrency holders and raises urgent questions about the physical security of global retail distribution channels. The specific malicious circuit utilized an STM32G431KBU6 chip and a shielded LTE module to transmit signals, with keys stolen via OCR reading of the device screen. Estimates indicate the supply-chain breach compromised approximately 300 wallets and resulted in up to $93.2 million in stolen funds across various blockchains.

**Background**: Hardware wallets are physical devices designed to securely store the cryptographic keys that control cryptocurrency assets, keeping them offline for enhanced protection against digital theft. A supply chain attack is a malicious strategy where third-party components or physical devices are compromised before they are delivered to the end consumer. This incident demonstrates that standard software defenses cannot protect a user if the hardware device itself has been physically altered and modified by a bad actor.

## [Timescale releases pg_vectorsearch for faster Postgres vector indexing](https://github.com/timescale/pg_vectorsearch) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Timescale has released pg_vectorsearch, a new PostgreSQL extension that claims to be faster than pgvector's HNSW and IVFFlat indexes by utilizing a quantized PRISM algorithm. The extension specifically optimizes vector search performance for modern NVMe storage environments. This release challenges the dominance of the standard pgvector library and offers a cost-effective solution for 'RAMageddon', where low-memory servers rely on fast disks for vector data. It allows engineers to optimize vector database costs while maintaining the relational integrity of PostgreSQL. The extension has critical limitations: it supports a maximum of 1968 vector dimensions and requires PostgreSQL version 18 or higher. Users with high-dimensional data or legacy database versions face migration challenges or feature lockouts.

hackernews · jascha_eng · Oct 14, 11:47 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50086165)

**Background**: Vector databases use indexes like HNSW (graph-based, high memory) or IVFFlat (partition-based, lower memory) to speed up similarity searches. In resource-constrained environments, known as 'RAMageddon', traditional in-memory indexes are too expensive, necessitating disk-optimized approaches that leverage fast NVMe drives to minimize latency.
