## [SK hynix: Advanced Packaging Becomes Central to HBM Evolution](https://semiwiki.com/semiconductor-manufacturers/372849-hbms-vertical-ascent-why-packaging-is-becoming-the-heart-of-ai-memory/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

SK hynix's Hot Chips 2026 presentation detailed how High Bandwidth Memory must evolve beyond incremental DRAM circuit improvements, with advanced packaging and denser vertical integration now positioned as the central drivers for meeting AI's escalating memory requirements. HBM has become the physical and economic bottleneck of the global AI industry in 2026, meaning that packaging innovation — not just transistor scaling — now dictates how fast AI infrastructure can advance and who controls the supply chain. HBM relies on 3D-stacked DRAM dies interconnected through through-silicon vias (TSVs) and co-located with GPUs on a custom silicon interposer; future bandwidth gains will therefore depend more on stacking height, bonding techniques, and thermal management within the package than on process-node shrinks alone.

rss · SemiWiki · Sep 3, 13:00

**Background**: High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) is a 3D-stacked DRAM architecture originally co-developed by Samsung, AMD, and SK Hynix. It stacks multiple DRAM dies vertically with TSVs acting as vertical interconnects, and sits physically adjacent to processors such as GPUs on a silicon interposer to deliver far higher bandwidth in a smaller footprint than conventional DRAM. This tight co-location is what makes HBM indispensable for AI accelerators, which must shuttle massive datasets between compute and memory at extremely high rates. As AI models continue to scale, the industry has found that simply shrinking transistors yields diminishing returns, so stacking more memory layers and improving the package itself have become the primary levers for boosting capacity and bandwidth.

## [Verisign Proposes Terminating the Entire .name TLD](https://neil.fraser.name/news/2026/09/03/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Verisign has proposed to terminate the .name top-level domain (TLD) entirely, which would eliminate all existing domain registrations under this TLD rather than merely ceasing new registrations. The proposal has raised significant concerns about internet governance, domain preservation, and the rights of current registrants. This proposal affects all current .name registrants globally and raises fundamental questions about the stability of internet infrastructure—domains that people have relied on for years could simply vanish. It also touches on ICANN's regulatory authority over registry operators and whether a single registry operator can unilaterally decommission an entire TLD without adequate registrant protections. The .name TLD has a unique three-level structure (e.g., first.last.name) where registrants can obtain third-level domains without owning the second-level domain, which has implications for browser cookie scoping and security models. The proposal does not mention preserving second-level domains to prevent domain squatting after termination, a concern raised by commentators.

hackernews · pavel_lishin · Sep 3, 14:54 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49550772)

**Background**: The Domain Name System (DNS) uses a hierarchical structure with top-level domains (TLDs) at the highest level, such as .com, .org, and .name. Verisign is a global provider of critical internet infrastructure and serves as the registry operator for several TLDs including .com and .net. ICANN (Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers) is the California-based nonprofit that oversees the domain name system and manages contracts with registry operators. The .name TLD was originally intended for personal names and uniquely allows registration at the third level (e.g., john.smith.name), making its structure fundamentally different from most other TLDs.
