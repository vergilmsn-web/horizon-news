## [Unofficial Mod Brings DLSS Multi Frame Generation to RTX 40-Series GPUs](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/we-tested-dlss-multi-frame-generation-on-rtx-40-series-gpus-new-mod-brings-rtx-50-series-exclusive-feature-to-older-cards-and-it-really-works) ⭐️ 7.5/10

Tom's Hardware tested an unofficial mod called 'MFGAdaUnlock-RenoDx' that successfully enables DLSS Multi Frame Generation—a feature previously exclusive to RTX 50-series GPUs—on older RTX 40-series hardware. The mod uses a ReShade add-on to deliver 3x, 4x and above frame generation, and testing in Cyberpunk 2077 confirmed that MFG is genuinely working on Ada Lovelace architecture cards. This finding has significant implications for millions of RTX 40-series GPU owners who were previously denied access to NVIDIA's latest frame-generation technology, potentially extending the useful life of their hardware. It also raises questions about NVIDIA's hardware-level feature segmentation strategy and highlights the ongoing cat-and-mouse dynamic between NVIDIA and the modding community. The mod leverages a ReShade-based injection layer rather than modifying the DLSS driver directly, and NVIDIA has already attempted to block it once before modders immediately re-unlocked it. NVIDIA warns that MFG on RTX 40 is unofficial and lacks the same testing and support as the RTX 50-series implementation, meaning users may encounter instability or image-quality issues.

rss · Tom's Hardware · Sep 12, 14:08

**Background**: DLSS (Deep Learning Super Sampling) is NVIDIA's AI-driven upscaling and frame-generation technology that boosts gaming performance by generating additional frames between traditionally rendered ones. DLSS 4, released on January 30, 2025, introduced Multi Frame Generation (MFG), which generates multiple AI frames per traditionally rendered frame and can multiply frame rates by up to 6.1x at 1440p on RTX 50-series GPUs. NVIDIA restricted MFG to its new Blackwell-based RTX 50-series cards, likely because the feature relies on new hardware capabilities in those GPUs, though this mod suggests the underlying Ada Lovelace architecture may be more capable than officially advertised.

## [Apple's A20 Pro 2nm Chip Shatters Geekbench 7 Single-Core Record](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cpus/apples-a20-pro-shatters-geekbench-7-single-core-record-2nm-chip-beats-desktop-intel-core-i9-and-amd-ryzen-9-by-up-to-32-percent) ⭐️ 7.5/10

Apple's upcoming A20 Pro smartphone SoC, fabricated on a 2nm process node, has reportedly achieved a record-breaking Geekbench 7 single-core score, outperforming desktop Intel Core i9 and AMD Ryzen 9 processors by up to 32%. This milestone demonstrates that ARM-based mobile silicon has decisively caught up to—and surpassed—high-end desktop x86 chips in single-threaded workloads, validating Apple's vertical-integration strategy and intensifying competitive pressure on Intel and AMD. The figures are based on pre-release or leaked benchmarks rather than independent verification, and Geekbench synthetic scores do not always correlate directly with real-world performance; furthermore, the comparison covers single-core only, while desktop chips typically retain advantages in multi-core workloads due to higher core counts and greater thermal headroom.

rss · Tom's Hardware · Sep 12, 10:48

**Background**: The 2nm process node represents the most advanced generation of semiconductor fabrication technology, enabling higher transistor density, improved performance, and reduced power consumption. A System on Chip (SoC) integrates components such as CPU cores, GPU, memory controllers, and AI accelerators onto a single die, a design approach common in smartphones and increasingly adopted in laptops. Geekbench 7 is a cross-platform benchmark that measures single-core and multi-core CPU performance across workloads including video conferencing, streaming, and game physics, and recently added support for CUDA, OpenCL, Vulkan, and Metal GPU testing. Apple's silicon roadmap has expanded from the A-series powering iPhones to the M-series powering Macs, all built on the ARM architecture and manufactured by TSMC.

## [Engineer builds crypto day trader powered by simulated fly brain](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/engineer-turns-simulated-fly-brain-into-a-crypto-day-trader-posts-downloadable-sim-to-github-166-700-virtual-neurons-read-candlestick-charts-for-dopamine-hits) ⭐️ 5.5/10

A Coinbase engineer created 'Stonkfly,' an open-source crypto day-trading system driven by 116,700 simulated neurons modeled on the fruit fly brain, which learns to interpret candlestick charts using dopamine-based reward signals. The project has been published on GitHub for others to download and experiment with. The project demonstrates how large-scale biologically realistic neural simulations, once confined to neuroscience labs, can be repurposed for unconventional real-world tasks like financial trading. It also highlights the growing accessibility of whole-brain connectome data and spiking neural network simulators for hobbyists and tinkerers. Stonkfly uses a spiking neural network architecture inspired by the Drosophila connectome — a wiring diagram containing roughly 125,000–139,000 neurons and 50 million synaptic connections assembled by recent neuroscience efforts. Learning is driven by dopamine-modulated spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP), a biologically plausible rule where profitable trades act as reward signals that strengthen useful synaptic connections.

rss · Tom's Hardware · Sep 12, 10:00

**Background**: The Drosophila connectome is a complete map of all neurons and chemical synapses in the adult fruit fly brain, completed by large-scale neuroscience collaborations and published in 2024. Spiking neural networks (SNNs) are a class of neural network model that more closely mimics biological neurons by communicating via discrete electrical spikes rather than continuous values. Dopamine-modulated learning draws on real neuroscience, where dopamine release in the brain signals reward and helps animals — including fruit flies — associate actions with positive outcomes.

## [d-Matrix Joins NVIDIA NVLink Fusion Platform for Raptor AI Accelerators](https://www.servethehome.com/d-matrix-joins-the-nvidia-nvlink-fusion-platform/) ⭐️ 5.5/10

d-Matrix announced that it will adopt NVIDIA's NVLink Fusion platform to scale up and scale out its next-generation Raptor AI accelerators. The Raptor accelerator is d-Matrix's first product featuring its 3D In-Memory Compute (3DIMC) technology, targeting generative AI inference workloads. This partnership signals NVIDIA's expanding influence in the AI accelerator interconnect ecosystem, allowing third-party silicon vendors like d-Matrix to plug directly into NVIDIA's rack-scale infrastructure such as NVL72-style systems. It validates d-Matrix's novel 3D-DRAM-based in-memory compute approach by giving it access to NVIDIA's high-bandwidth, low-latency interconnect fabric for AI factory-scale deployments. Raptor leverages 3DIMC technology bundled with Andes RISC-V CPU IP (announced November 2025), and its 3D-DRAM architecture claims density and power-per-GB/s advantages over HBM4 and NVIDIA Rubin R200 according to d-Matrix's own benchmarks. NVLink Fusion extends NVIDIA's proprietary interconnect to third-party XPUs and CPUs from vendors like Marvell, Qualcomm, and MediaTek.

rss · ServeTheHome · Sep 12, 21:42

**Background**: NVIDIA NVLink is a high-speed proprietary interconnect that enables GPUs and accelerators to communicate with very high bandwidth and low latency, forming the backbone of NVIDIA's rack-scale AI systems like the NVL72. NVLink Fusion is NVIDIA's program to open this interconnect to third-party silicon, allowing custom XPUs, accelerators, and data center CPUs from other vendors to slot into NVIDIA's AI factory infrastructure. d-Matrix is a startup focused on generative AI inference compute, and its Raptor accelerator uses an unusual 3D-stacked DRAM approach (3DIMC) to bring memory bandwidth and capacity closer to the compute elements, aiming to address the memory bottleneck that limits large language model inference throughput.
