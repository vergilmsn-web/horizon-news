## [China begins mass production of homegrown immersion DUV lithography machines](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/china-begins-mass-production-of-domestic-immersion-duv-lithography-machines) ⭐️ 9.5/10

Shanghai Yuliangsheng Technology, a state-backed Shanghai company, has reportedly begun limited mass production of immersion DUV lithography machines, with the first five units scheduled for delivery this year to SMIC, Hua Hong, and CXMT. The company reportedly aims to produce approximately 20 immersion DUV machines by 2027. This represents a potential breakthrough in reducing China's dependency on ASML's near-monopoly on advanced lithography equipment and could undermine the effectiveness of US-led export controls targeting China's chip manufacturing capabilities. If verified, the move could reshape the global semiconductor supply chain and intensify geopolitical competition over chip-making technology. Immersion DUV machines employ an argon-fluoride excimer laser to produce 193nm wavelength light through a water immersion layer for enhanced resolution. For context, ASML shipped 131 immersion DUV machines in 2024, highlighting that Chinese production remains at a smaller scale. The Chinese systems still import some key components from Japan, though the majority are now domestically sourced.

rss · Tom's Hardware · Jul 27, 16:51

**Background**: Lithography machines are essential tools in chip manufacturing, used to print intricate circuit patterns onto silicon wafers. Immersion DUV lithography enhances resolution by placing a thin layer of ultra-purified water between the final lens and the wafer, allowing finer feature sizes than conventional dry lithography. ASML, the Dutch company, has been the dominant global supplier of advanced lithography systems, producing both DUV and the more advanced EUV machines. The United States and its allies have imposed export restrictions on leading-edge semiconductor equipment to China, particularly EUV and most advanced DUV systems, driving China's push to develop domestic alternatives.

Moonshot AI is a Beijing-based AI startup founded in 2023 that quickly rose to prominence with its Kimi chatbot and Kimi K2 model. ## [TSMC Hits 20,000 Wafers Per Month Milestone on 2nm N2 Node](https://www.techpowerup.com/351117/tsmc-hits-20-000-wafers-per-month-milestone-on-2-nm-node) ⭐️ 7.5/10

TSMC has reached a production milestone of 20,000 wafers per month on its 2nm N2 node, with high-volume manufacturing at Fab 20 in Taiwan having begun in Q4 2025. The N2 node has attracted four times more customer tape-outs than the previous N3 node, including AMD's EPYC "Venice" server CPUs and Apple's A20 Pro chips for the iPhone 18 Pro series. This milestone signals the beginning of the industry's transition to the 2nm era, with GAAFET technology delivering meaningful efficiency gains (15% performance at same power or 30% power reduction at same speed). Despite N2 currently representing only 3% of revenue, the fourfold tape-out growth compared to N3 suggests it will become the dominant node over the coming years. N2 currently accounts for just 3% of TSMC's revenue, while N3 holds 30% and N5 leads at 33%, showing the early ramp stage of the new node. TSMC operates five fabs in Taiwan allocated for N2 manufacturing, though only Fab 20 has reached volume production at the 20,000 wafers/month figure as of now.

rss · TechPowerUp News · Jul 27, 17:06

**Background**: Tape-out is the final step in IC design before a chip is sent to a foundry for manufacturing, marking the point at which the design data is finalized and transferred to create photomasks for production. GAAFET (Gate-All-Around FET) is an advanced transistor architecture that replaces the FinFET design used in earlier nodes; by wrapping the gate completely around the channel, it provides better electrostatic control, reduces leakage, and enables further transistor scaling. TSMC's N2 is one of the first high-volume nodes to deploy GAAFET technology, following Samsung's earlier 3nm GAA node and competing with Intel's upcoming 18A process.

## [Developer Runs 28.9M-Parameter LLM on Sub-$10 ESP32-S3 Microcontroller](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/ai-developer-runs-28-9-million-parameter-model-on-usd10-esp32-s3-microcontroller-uses-googles-per-layer-embeddings-technique-stores-table-on-16mb-flash-memory) ⭐️ 7.5/10

A developer has successfully run a 28.9-million-parameter language model on a sub-$10 ESP32-S3 microcontroller using Google's Per-Layer Embeddings (PLE) technique, with the lookup table stored in 16MB of external flash memory. This demonstrates that meaningful AI inference can occur on hardware costing roughly the price of a fast-food meal. The achievement lowers the barrier for deploying language models on cheap, low-power edge devices, opening doors for offline AI assistants in IoT products, embedded systems, and scenarios where cloud connectivity is unavailable or undesirable. It illustrates how algorithmic innovations like Per-Layer Embeddings can dramatically reduce memory requirements, potentially reshaping how engineers approach on-device AI. The ESP32-S3 has only 512KB of internal SRAM, so the 28.9M-parameter weights are not held in memory directly; instead, the lookup table is stored in 16MB of flash and accessed via the PLE technique, which compresses embedding tables on a per-layer basis. The trade-off is a likely drop in inference speed (tokens per second) and possibly some accuracy compared to running the model on a desktop GPU.

rss · Tom's Hardware · Jul 27, 13:07

**Background**: The ESP32-S3 is a dual-core Xtensa LX7 microcontroller from Espressif Systems that runs at up to 240 MHz and integrates Wi-Fi 4 and Bluetooth 5 (LE), commonly used in IoT prototyping where cost and power are priorities. Per-Layer Embeddings (PLE) is a Google research technique designed to shrink the memory footprint of large language models by compressing embedding tables on a per-layer basis, letting models that normally need gigabytes of RAM fit into much smaller storage. Edge AI is the broader concept of running machine-learning inference on local devices rather than in the cloud, which reduces latency, preserves privacy, and enables offline operation.
