## [Anthropic Hit with Record $1.5B Copyright Settlement](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-slapped-with-usd1-5-billion-settlement-in-copyright-lawsuit-largest-payout-ever-court-says-that-training-ai-on-books-other-publications-is-fair-use-but-ruled-that-the-startups-7-million-book-pirated-library-infringes-authors-rights) ⭐️ 8.5/10

A U.S. federal judge approved a landmark $1.5 billion settlement between Anthropic and a class of authors, the largest payout ever in an AI copyright case. The court ruled that training AI on legitimately published material qualifies as fair use, but concluded that Anthropic's maintenance of a pirated library of approximately 7 million books constitutes copyright infringement. This ruling establishes a critical legal precedent separating the act of training on public, published works—which is permitted—from acquiring and storing those works through piracy, which is not. It directly affects how every major AI company sources, curates, and stores its training corpora, and sets a binding reference point for the dozens of similar lawsuits pending against OpenAI, Meta, and others. The settlement amount was accepted by a majority of plaintiffs, but a minority refused, arguing that $1.5 billion is too small relative to the scale of infringement across roughly 7 million titles; those objectors are pursuing a separate lawsuit. The ruling is significant because the court drew an explicit line between ingesting publicly available text for model training and building a centralized archive of pirated source material.

rss · Tom's Hardware · Jul 21, 13:37

**Background**: Fair use is a U.S. legal doctrine codified in 17 U.S.C. § 107 that permits limited use of copyrighted material without the rights-holder's permission, weighing factors such as the purpose of the use, the nature of the work, the amount used, and the effect on the market. AI developers typically train large language models on massive text corpora that may include web scrapes, books, and encyclopedias (for example, GPT-3 disclosed training on Common Crawl, WebText2, books, and English Wikipedia). The Anthropic case hinged on a distinction the court found dispositive: whether the company merely ingested published text during training, or whether it also assembled and retained a 7-million-book library of pirated copies—a distinction with major implications for how datasets like Books3 have been assembled across the industry.

## [Man Restores Sperm Production After Transplanting Testicular Tissue Frozen 16 Years in Childhood](https://www.solidot.org/story?sid=84878) ⭐️ 8.3/10

A medical team at Université Libre de Bruxelles Hospital in Belgium successfully transplanted 11 testicular tissue fragments—frozen in 2008 when the patient was a 10-year-old boy facing chemotherapy for sickle cell disease—back into the now-adult man, restoring active spermatogenesis. Analysis after one year detected spermatogonial stem cells and signs of active sperm generation across multiple grafts, including a single mature sperm in one sample. This marks the first confirmed case in humans of functional spermatogenesis restored from long-term cryopreserved pre-pubertal testicular tissue, offering a viable fertility-preservation pathway for the tens of thousands of children who undergo gonadotoxic treatments such as chemotherapy each year. It validates a strategy that, until now, had only been successfully demonstrated in animals, and could reshape clinical guidelines for fertility counseling before pediatric cancer or sickle cell treatment. Eleven tissue fragments were transplanted into both the remaining testis and the subcutaneous scrotal space, and were cultured for a full year under adult hormonal conditions before analysis. While the presence of spermatogonial stem cells and one mature sperm is a milestone, the procedure remains experimental, with conception and live-birth outcomes yet to be documented; more than 3,000 testicular samples are currently cryopreserved worldwide under similar protocols.

rss · Solidot · Jul 20, 16:33

**Background**: Chemotherapy and hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, while life-saving for conditions such as sickle cell disease and pediatric cancers, often cause irreversible damage to reproductive organs. For adult males, sperm banking before treatment is straightforward, but pre-pubertal boys cannot produce sperm, so cryopreservation of testicular tissue—hoping that future medicine could restore fertility—has been offered experimentally for years. Direct tissue transplantation is currently considered the most promising strategy for using cryopreserved testicular tissue to restore male fertility, though it remains unproven in humans until now.

Concern is growing over undisclosed debt being taken on by hyperscalers to fund AI datacenters whose server hardware follows an extremely short 18-36 month replacement cycle, raising sustainability questions about these massive infrastructure investments. This matters because the combination of off-balance-sheet financing and rapid hardware obsolescence creates a structural financial risk: companies are locking in long-term debt obligations for assets that may lose competitive value in under three years, potentially exposing investors and the broader AI ecosystem to significant losses if AI monetization stalls. According to related reports, more than $120 billion of AI data center financing has been shifted into Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs) backed by private credit and project finance markets, and hyperscalers' off-balance-sheet obligations—primarily lease commitments and chip purchase contracts—are now approaching $1 trillion, contractual fixed payments that could weaken balance sheets if AI infrastructure monetization fails.

rss · Electronics Weekly · Jul 21, 05:03

**Background**: Hyperscalers are companies such as Amazon Web Services, Microsoft, Google, Meta, Oracle, and Alibaba that operate enormous data centers capable of scaling to serve global application demand. The recent AI boom has driven unprecedented capital expenditure on GPU clusters and specialized AI hardware. To fund this buildout while preserving their investment-grade credit ratings, these companies have increasingly used off-balance-sheet structures such as SPVs and sale-leaseback arrangements, which keep debt off their main balance sheets. Meanwhile, unlike traditional servers that could serve useful purposes for 5-7 years, AI accelerators like GPUs become obsolete much faster as newer, more efficient chips are released every 18-36 months, creating a unique mismatch between asset lifespan and debt duration.
