The detector uses a cubic-kilometer of Antarctic ice to capture Cherenkov radiation produced when neutrinos interact with matter, a mechanism that relies on charged particles moving faster than light in the medium.

## [Gleam Compiler Now Generates Erlang Abstract Forms Instead of Source Code](https://gleam.run/news/gleam-doesnt-compile-to-erlang-source-anymore/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Gleam has updated its compiler pipeline to stop generating Erlang source code as an intermediate step. Instead, it now compiles directly to Erlang Abstract Forms, a lower-level AST representation used by the Erlang compiler itself. This architectural change improves tooling efficiency and maturity by aligning with how modern BEAM languages like Elixir compile. It reduces overhead and allows for better integration with the Erlang ecosystem's internal structures. The new compiler output format is Erlang Abstract Forms, which are canonically made of Erlang terms and manipulated via standard library routines. This approach is preferred over direct BEAM bytecode generation because BEAM instructions change with every VM release, requiring continuous tracking of decades of Erlang compiler optimizations.

hackernews · ingve · Oct 6, 08:08 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49975619)

**Background**: Gleam is a functional, statically-typed programming language that compiles to the BEAM virtual machine. Traditionally, it generated human-readable Erlang source code, which was then compiled by the Erlang compiler. Erlang Abstract Forms represent the intermediate AST structure within the Erlang compiler, similar to the target used by Elixir's compile-time macro system.

## [Q Labs Releases Dust for Pretraining Transformers Without Backprop](https://qlabs.sh/research/dust) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Q Labs introduced Dust, a method that pretrains transformer language models using zeroth-order optimization instead of backpropagation. Experiments show that with a large population of activation perturbations, Dust can approach the performance of backpropagation. This research explores an alternative optimization paradigm that could be valuable in scenarios where gradients are unavailable or weak, such as with discontinuous objectives. It challenges the conventional reliance on backpropagation in deep learning training pipelines. The method is significantly more compute-intensive than backpropagation and operates in a 'compute-rich' regime where it might surpass standard methods. It is specifically distinguished from weight-space evolutionary strategies by being orders of magnitude more efficient.

hackernews · E-Reverance · Oct 5, 21:15 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49970871)

**Background**: Backpropagation is a standard first-order algorithm that uses explicit gradients to update neural network weights efficiently. Zeroth-order optimization estimates directional improvements by measuring changes in function values rather than calculating derivatives directly. This study introduces 'Dust' as a framework for applying this gradient-free approach specifically to the pretraining of large-scale transformer models.

A new research model demonstrates that nature's ability to recover from species loss is significantly less robust than previously thought. This model provides a global picture to predict the benefits biodiversity offers, challenging the traditional concept of natural equilibrium. This finding is critical for conservation efforts because it debunks the misconception that ecosystems will naturally repair themselves after disruption. It shifts the focus from passive restoration to active management and quantifying the specific, location-based benefits of maintaining biodiversity. The model allows for the prediction of biodiversity benefits anywhere in the world, enabling targeted conservation strategies. It highlights that recovery timescales are often much longer than human planning horizons, with some ecosystems failing to return to previous states even after centuries.

hackernews · pseudolus · Oct 6, 11:11 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49976823)

**Background**: For decades, ecological management relied on the 'equilibrium' model, which assumes nature maintains a stable, self-correcting balance that returns to its original state after minor disturbances. Modern 'nonequilibrium' theory argues that ecosystems are dynamic and constantly changing, and that once a species is lost, it often opens niches for other species (like invasive ones or different prey) rather than simply waiting for the original species to return.
