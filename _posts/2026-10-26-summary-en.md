Micron has announced plans to bypass the standard 4F2 DRAM roadmap by integrating wafer bonding and High-NA EUV lithography to increase memory density. The company also intends to pursue 3D NAND and vertical memory architectures in the coming years. This strategic shift is significant because it could break the traditional scaling limits of 2D planar DRAM, potentially accelerating the pace of memory density improvements. It connects to broader industry trends where manufacturers must adopt advanced lithography and 3D stacking to meet the exploding demands of AI and data centers. The approach relies on High-NA EUV, an extremely advanced lithography tool that resolves features much smaller than current standard EUV, combined with wafer bonding to vertically stack memory cells. The plan explicitly departs from the 4F2 node transition, which has been the industry standard for DRAM scaling for over a decade.

rss · Tom's Hardware · Oct 26, 14:00

**Background**: 4F2 refers to a specific manufacturing metric where a DRAM cell is only four gate pitches wide and two tall, marking the physical limit of 2D scaling. To build smaller, denser memory beyond this limit, companies are looking at High-NA EUV (extreme ultraviolet light with a high numerical aperture) to print smaller masks, or adopting wafer bonding to stack multiple layers of cells vertically. This vertical integration mimics the 3D approach used in NAND flash but applied to DRAM.

Panel-level processing is emerging as a critical manufacturing path to scale advanced chip packaging for AI workloads. This shift moves the industry from traditional circular wafers to square or rectangular panels to meet growing demands. The increase in die sizes and I/O counts required by AI chips makes traditional wafer-level packaging economically and technically constrained. Adopting panel-level packaging improves area utilization and scalability, which is vital for reducing costs and accelerating semiconductor supply. Panel-level packaging utilizes square or rectangular substrates, such as glass, which allows for better area utilization compared to round wafers. This approach often aligns with fan-out packaging, offering a lower cost alternative to silicon interposers while supporting high I/O counts.

rss · EE Times · Oct 26, 13:00

**Background**: Advanced packaging refers to integrating multiple chips or die on a single substrate to overcome the physical limits of scaling transistors within a single die. While traditional manufacturing relies on processing standard circular wafers, larger AI processors require more real estate, leading to the development of panel-level techniques that process larger, square arrays of chips.
