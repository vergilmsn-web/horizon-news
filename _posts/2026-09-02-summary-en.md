## [TSMC Bets on 5μm Microbumps Instead of Hybrid Bonding](https://www.techpowerup.com/352267/tsmc-skips-hybrid-bonding-for-now-betting-on-conventional-microbumps-to-lead-the-way) ⭐️ 7.5/10

TSMC is reportedly skipping hybrid bonding technology and instead preparing its supply chain for 5-micrometer microbumps, with mass production targeted for the second half of 2028. The company continues to rely on its current 20-micrometer microbump approach for CoWoS-S and CoWoS-L packaging while limiting its hybrid bonding capacity. This decision directly impacts the AI chip manufacturing landscape, as CoWoS packaging is the critical interconnect technology that ties GPUs to HBM memory in AI accelerators from NVIDIA and others. TSMC's microbump-focused strategy diverges from AMD's hybrid bonding approach used in 3D V-Cache, shaping the performance and density trade-offs available to chip designers through at least 2028. Traditional solder bumps measure around 100μm, current microbumps about 20μm, and hybrid bonding reduces pitch to roughly 1μm with direct copper-to-copper connections. TSMC's 5μm microbump target would shrink current bumps by 4x while sidestepping the manufacturing complexity of hybrid bonding, though its existing hybrid bonding capacity—which produces AMD's 3D V-Cache SRAM-on-logic stacks—will reportedly remain constrained.

rss · TechPowerUp News · Sep 2, 15:43

**Background**: Advanced chip packaging has become increasingly important as transistor scaling slows, with TSMC's CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate) technology enabling multiple dies to be combined into a single package for higher performance. CoWoS comes in variants: CoWoS-S uses a silicon interposer for best-in-class signal integrity and bandwidth, while CoWoS-L uses an RDL-based interposer that supports larger packages more cost-effectively for AI workloads. Interconnects within these packages—traditionally solder bumps or microbumps—are critical bottlenecks, and hybrid bonding represents the next step by enabling direct copper-to-copper connections with much higher density and finer pitch.

## [Samsung Memory Roadmap: HBM5 Targets 2x Performance, zHBM Promises 8x](https://www.techpowerup.com/352233/samsung-new-memory-roadmap-hbm5-aims-for-2x-performance-zhbm-for-8x) ⭐️ 7.5/10

At SEMICON Taiwan 2026, Samsung unveiled its memory roadmap centering on HBM5 and the radical zHBM architecture. HBM5 targets 2x the performance and 20% better performance-per-watt over HBM4E, using Samsung's in-house 2nm base die (up from 4nm) with 12/16/20-layer stack options and mass production slated for around 2028. zHBM, which stacks memory directly on top of the processor rather than beside it, promises 8x the raw performance and 3x the performance-per-watt of HBM4E, with a post-2029 launch target. HBM is the primary memory technology powering today's AI accelerators, and Samsung's roadmap directly impacts how much compute can be fed by memory bandwidth—the key bottleneck for large language model training and inference. The shift to zHBM represents a fundamental architectural rethinking that could reshape how AI chips are designed, potentially enabling AI accelerators with aggregated memory bandwidth of around 100 TB/s per system. HBM5 is expected to deliver approximately 4 TB/s of bandwidth per stack, enabling AI accelerators with aggregated bandwidth around 100 TB/s. Thermal resistance is reduced by 20% in HBM5 and by 75–90% in zHBM. Samsung is currently shipping HBM4 at 11.7 Gbps/pin and confirmed HBM4E samples running at 16 Gbps/pin at Hot Chips 2026. NVIDIA has separately announced its own NVHBM approach, which moves the memory controller into the HBM base die rather than keeping it on the compute die, indicating an industry-wide architectural shift.

rss · TechPowerUp News · Sep 1, 19:09

**Background**: High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) is a 3D-stacked DRAM technology that achieves much higher bandwidth than traditional memory like DDR5 or GDDR by using a wide data path and vertical through-silicon vias (TSVs). Conventional HBM sits beside the compute die (e.g., a GPU) on a silicon interposer, connected via the base die. The base die contains logic that manages the interface between the stacked DRAM and the host processor. Each new HBM generation typically increases capacity (more stack layers), speed (Gbps per pin), and bandwidth, and is critical for AI workloads where memory bandwidth often bottlenecks compute throughput. zHBM represents a more radical departure by physically placing memory above the processor rather than next to it, dramatically shortening the data path.

**Background**: High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) is a 3D-stacked DRAM technology that sits directly on the GPU package via advanced packaging, offering extremely high bandwidth but limited capacity (typically tens of GB per stack) at high cost.
