## [Qualcomm Validates HBC Silicon for 2027 Dragonfly AI250](https://www.techpowerup.com/354409/qualcomm-validates-hbc-silicon-samples-appear-in-first-half-of-2027) ⭐️ 8.5/10

Qualcomm has successfully validated its High Bandwidth Compute (HBC) silicon, which stacks LPDDR layers on a base compute die for AI inference. Samples of the associated Dragonfly AI250 chip are expected in the first half of 2027. This milestone moves Qualcomm's novel memory-compute integration from concept to physical proof, offering a more energy-efficient alternative to traditional HBM for edge and mobile AI workloads. By reducing data movement, it promises higher bandwidth per watt for battery-constrained devices. The first-generation HBC achieved silicon validation with full read-write and near-memory computation capabilities in Qualcomm's San Diego lab. The architecture claims a 6x increase in bandwidth per watt compared to the current HBM specification by embedding compute directly under the memory stacks.

rss · TechPowerUp News · Nov 2, 16:11

**Background**: High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) typically stacks standard DDR chips using Through-Silicon Vias (TSVs) to increase bandwidth, but this is power-intensive. Qualcomm's HBC uses low-power LPDDR chips instead, stacking them vertically over a logic die that performs near-memory computation. This approach aims to combine the bandwidth benefits of 3D stacking with the energy efficiency of LPDDR, specifically for AI inference tasks.

## [Subvocal Launches RF-Sensing Smart Glasses for Silent Speech](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50340088) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The Subvocal Company has announced consumer smart glasses that use radio frequency (RF) sensing to decode silent subvocalization into text. The founders pivoted from unreliable EMG electrodes to an RF-based system that reads internal speech movements without moving the lips or making sound. This device enables hands-free and quiet dictation in noise-sensitive environments like libraries and offices, addressing a key limitation of current voice assistants. It pushes the frontier of human-computer interaction and raises significant privacy questions about silent data capture. The system operates by emitting RF waves that pass through the face and are modulated by the tongue and lips, which are then decoded using foundation models. The technology can even detect 'internal speech' where the user mouths words with a closed mouth, although it is not automatic unconscious thinking.

hackernews · aarushyadav · Nov 2, 17:16

**Background**: Subvocalization involves silently moving the speech organs to articulate words without producing audible sound. While radio frequency sensing has been used in academic research like MIT's AlterEgo system for a decade, translating these lab experiments into a robust, wearable consumer product requires overcoming signal interference and ensuring it works across users with different facial anatomy.
