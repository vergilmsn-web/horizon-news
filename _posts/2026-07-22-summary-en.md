## [Anthropic Fined Record $1.5B in Copyright Settlement](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-slapped-with-usd1-5-billion-settlement-in-copyright-lawsuit-largest-payout-ever-court-says-that-training-ai-on-books-other-publications-is-fair-use-but-ruled-that-the-startups-7-million-book-pirated-library-infringes-authors-rights) ⭐️ 9.5/10

A U.S. federal judge approved a landmark $1.5 billion settlement against Anthropic in the Bartz v. Anthropic copyright lawsuit, the largest-ever payout in a case involving an AI company. The court ruled that training AI on lawfully published books constitutes fair use, but Anthropic's use of a pirated 7-million-book library constitutes copyright infringement. This ruling establishes a critical judicial distinction that will shape how all AI companies source training data: training on legitimate published material is permissible, but using pirated libraries is not. The precedent pressures AI firms to vet their data provenance and could trigger similar litigation against other companies suspected of using shadow libraries such as LibGen. Eligible authors will receive approximately $3,000 per infringed title, and the judge slashed the class counsel's fee from 12.5% ($187.5 million) to 6.8% ($101 million). A minority of plaintiffs rejected the settlement as too small and are pursuing a separate lawsuit of their own.

**Background**: The case, Bartz v. Anthropic, was presided over by Judge William Alsup of the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, who issued a notable 2025 ruling applying the fair use doctrine to AI training for the first time. The fair use doctrine, codified in U.S. copyright law, permits limited use of copyrighted material without permission under factors including the purpose of use, the nature of the work, the amount used, and the effect on the market. Shadow libraries like Library Genesis (LibGen) and Sci-Hub are vast online repositories of pirated books and articles; court documents have suggested that multiple AI companies, including Meta and potentially OpenAI, used LibGen-sourced material to train their models.

## [NVIDIA Reveals Rubin GPU Architecture: 50 PFLOPS NVFP4, 288GB HBM4](https://www.techpowerup.com/350947/nvidia-shares-rubin-gpu-deep-dive-and-die-annotation) ⭐️ 8.5/10

NVIDIA has published a deep-dive into its next-generation 'Rubin' GPU architecture, revealing a chip with 224 streaming multiprocessors (SMs), 336 billion transistors, 896 Tensor Cores with a third-generation Transformer Engine, and 288 GB of HBM4 memory. The full-configuration accelerator delivers up to 50 PetaFLOPS of compute at sparse NVFP4 precision and connects via NVLink 6 at 3,600 GB/s and PCIe Gen 6 at 256 GB/s. Rubin represents a major generational leap in NVIDIA's data-center GPU lineup, with HBM4 bandwidth, NVLink 6 fabric, and NVFP4 throughput collectively reshaping the economics of training and serving large AI models. The architecture's scale-up and scale-out capabilities will influence how hyperscalers, cloud providers, and enterprises design AI infrastructure for the next several years. The 288 GB of HBM4 is implemented using 12-high modules paired with dedicated controllers and PHYs to reach up to 22 TB/s of peak memory bandwidth, while a redesigned Tensor Memory Accelerator improves data-movement efficiency. On the GPU itself, work is organized into Graphics Processor Clusters (GPCs) with a centralized L2 cache, scheduled by a GigaThread Engine, and partitioned by MIG into multiple isolated virtual GPUs.

rss · TechPowerUp News · Jul 21, 18:57

**Background**: NVFP4 is a 4-bit floating-point format that NVIDIA introduced with the Blackwell architecture, using a two-level hardware scaling mechanism so that ultra-low-precision values can safely represent the wide dynamic range of neural-network activations and weights. HBM4 is the sixth generation of JEDEC's High Bandwidth Memory standard, doubling the per-stack interface to 2048 bits and pushing per-stack bandwidth above 1.6–2.8 TB/s depending on the vendor's signaling rate. NVIDIA MIG (Multi-Instance GPU) is a hardware feature that partitions a single GPU into up to seven fully isolated instances, each with dedicated compute and memory, enabling multi-tenant and multi-user use in virtualized environments.

## [SMIC's N+3 7nm Process Rivals TSMC N6 Density Without EUV](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/smics-third-gen-7nm-node-shows-smaller-metal-pitch-than-intel-18a-higher-transistor-density-than-tsmc-n6-without-euv-analysis-of-n-3-shows-significant-advancement-for-chinese-semi-manufacturing) ⭐️ 8.5/10

Analysis of SMIC's third-generation 7nm node (N+3) reveals it achieves a smaller metal pitch than Intel 18A and higher transistor density than TSMC's N6 process, all while relying exclusively on DUV multi-patterning lithography instead of EUV. This demonstrates a significant technical leap for China's domestic semiconductor manufacturing under US export controls that block access to EUV lithography systems from ASML, potentially allowing Chinese fabs to continue advancing node technology despite sanctions, though with efficiency trade-offs. SMIC achieves these density metrics through aggressive DUV multi-patterning, which is slower and less yield-efficient than single-exposure EUV; consequently, N+3 does not match the performance or power efficiency of modern production nodes from TSMC or Samsung. The metal pitch measurement and transistor density figures come from independent die analysis rather than official SMIC specifications.

rss · Tom's Hardware · Jul 21, 11:00

**Background**: EUV (extreme ultraviolet) lithography uses 13.5nm wavelength light to print extremely fine circuit patterns on silicon wafers in a single exposure, enabling leading-edge nodes like TSMC's N5 and N3. DUV (deep ultraviolet) lithography operates at a longer 193nm wavelength and cannot natively achieve the same resolution, but multi-patterning techniques—where multiple DUV exposures are combined—can simulate finer features at the cost of throughput and yield. Metal pitch refers to the minimum spacing between metal interconnect lines on a chip, and smaller pitches generally enable higher transistor density. TSMC's N6 is a 6nm-class refinement of its 7nm process, while Intel 18A (roughly 1.8nm-class) represents Intel's most advanced node. SMIC, China's largest foundry, has been barred from acquiring EUV systems due to US sanctions, making its DUV-based progress strategically significant.
