## [China Successfully Lands Long March 10B Reusable Rocket](https://www.electronicsweekly.com/news/china-gets-space-boost-of-landing-reusable-rocket-2026-07/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

On July 10, 2026, China successfully launched and recovered the Long March 10B reusable rocket, with its first stage booster performing a propulsive landing into a retrieval net system at the Hainan Commercial Space Launch Site. The rocket's maiden flight also placed an unnamed satellite into orbit, making the Long March 10B only the fifth orbital-class rocket system worldwide to achieve a successful propulsive recovery. This milestone marks China's entry into the reusable launch vehicle arena, a capability previously dominated by SpaceX and a handful of other players. Reusability dramatically lowers per-launch costs and increases flight cadence, which has transformative implications for satellite deployment, crewed spaceflight (including China's planned lunar missions), and the broader competitive dynamics of the global space industry. The Long March 10B shares the same first-stage booster as the Long March 10A (which is still awaiting its first full-scale test flight) but features a different upper stage and payload fairing optimized for cargo and satellites. Recovery was achieved using a net-based capture system rather than landing legs, an approach that differs from SpaceX's Falcon 9 drone-ship or ground-landing technique.

rss · Electronics Weekly · Jul 13, 05:18

**Background**: A reusable launch vehicle (RLV) is a rocket system whose stages can be recovered and reflown, as opposed to traditional expendable rockets that are discarded after a single use. The concept dramatically reduces launch costs, since the most expensive components—engines and avionics—can be reused multiple times. SpaceX pioneered orbital-class propulsive booster recovery with the Falcon 9, first achieving a successful landing in 2015, and has since made reusability a cornerstone of commercial spaceflight economics. The Long March 10B was developed by CALT (China Academy of Launch Vehicle Technology), a research institute under the state-owned China Aerospace Science and Technology Corporation (CASC), which is the primary contractor for China's space launch capabilities.

## [JEDEC SPHBM4 Standard Brings HBM4 Bandwidth to Organic Substrates](https://www.techpowerup.com/350725/jedec-sphbm4-standard-enables-hbm4-class-bandwidth-on-organic-substrates) ⭐️ 7.5/10

JEDEC has published the JESD330-4 SPHBM4 standard, which uses the same DRAM dies as HBM4 but adds a new interface base die that enables mounting on standard organic substrates rather than silicon interposers. The standard achieves equivalent aggregate bandwidth to HBM4 by reducing data signals from 2048 to 512 using 4:1 serialization at a higher operating frequency, allowing the relaxed bump pitch required for organic substrate connections. This standardization addresses a key cost bottleneck in deploying HBM4 for AI accelerators, since silicon interposers and advanced packaging (like CoWoS) are expensive and capacity-constrained. By enabling organic substrate integration, SPHBM4 could lower packaging costs, ease supply chain pressure, and broaden HBM4 adoption beyond the highest-end accelerators that can absorb current packaging costs. The 4:1 serialization trades pin count for frequency: only 512 data signals are needed instead of 2048, but they must run at four times the rate to preserve bandwidth. The same DRAM dies are reused, meaning the standard is primarily an interface and packaging adaptation rather than a new memory technology, which simplifies qualification and manufacturing.

rss · TechPowerUp News · Jul 13, 15:34

**Background**: HBM4 is the sixth-generation High Bandwidth Memory standard, doubling the interface width to 2048 bits with 32 independent channels to deliver over 2.0 TB/s per stack. It is the dominant memory choice for AI accelerators from NVIDIA, AMD, and hyperscalers because traditional DRAM cannot keep modern matrix engines fed. However, HBM4's fine bump pitch typically requires silicon interposers or advanced packaging like TSMC's CoWoS, which are significantly more expensive and capacity-limited than organic substrates, creating a packaging bottleneck for the entire AI industry.

## [Intel 18A Silicon Achieves Space-Grade Certification with Starfire Processors](https://www.techpowerup.com/350713/intel-18a-silicon-goes-to-space-with-starfire-processors) ⭐️ 7.5/10

Intel's 18A process node has officially achieved space-grade certification with the new 'Starfire' family of processors designed for orbital computing. The lineup includes two SKUs — a Low Power variant (10W TDP, P-cores at 1.0 GHz) and a Performance variant (35W TDP, P-cores at 3.1 GHz) — both featuring a hybrid 4 P-core + 4 LPE-core design, an Intel 3 GPU tile with 4 Xe cores (64 EUs), and on-die NPUs (45–75 TOPS INT8), with Q3 2025 targeted for samples. This marks the first time Intel's most advanced 18A node has been qualified for space, opening a new high-margin market for Intel Foundry and validating the node's readiness beyond terrestrial use. It also strengthens Intel's strategic position in the growing orbital computing ecosystem, where radiation-hardened silicon is in increasing demand for satellite constellations and defense applications. Both Starfire SKUs operate across a junction-temperature range of −55°C to 125°C and carry TID, SEL, and SEE radiation-hardening certifications, making them suitable for harsh orbital environments. Notably, the GPU tile is fabricated on the older Intel 3 node rather than 18A, and the on-package NPU delivers up to 75 TOPS (INT8) on the Performance SKU, enabling on-board AI inference for space payloads.

rss · TechPowerUp News · Jul 13, 11:38

**Background**: Intel 18A is the company's most advanced manufacturing process and notably the first commercial foundry node to implement backside power delivery (Intel's PowerVia technology), which moves power-routing components behind the die to improve efficiency. Space-grade certification requires chips to withstand extreme temperatures and high-energy particle radiation, typically demanding radiation-hardened designs with TID (Total Ionizing Dose), SEL (Single Event Latch-up), and SEE (Single Event Effect) qualifications. Hybrid architectures like P-cores (high-performance) and LPE-cores (Low Power Efficient cores, located in the SoC tile for ultra-light background tasks) allow a single SoC to balance compute throughput with power savings — a critical tradeoff for power-constrained satellites where solar generation is limited.

TrendForce's latest memory pricing survey forecasts that SLC NAND flash prices will rise by 120–170% in the second half of 2026, driven by niche demand and the migration of mature NAND production capacity toward higher-value MLC and other advanced products. This sharp projected increase could significantly raise costs for embedded systems, industrial equipment, automotive electronics, and IoT devices that rely on SLC NAND for its superior endurance and reliability, potentially cascading through supply chains in the semiconductor and electronics industries. SLC (Single-Level Cell) NAND stores one bit per cell, offering the highest endurance and reliability among NAND types but at a higher cost per gigabyte; manufacturers are reallocating mature production lines to MLC, TLC, and QLC to maximize revenue density, which reduces SLC supply.

rss · DRAMeXchange (TrendForce) · Jul 13, 15:16

**Background**: NAND flash memory comes in several types based on how many bits each cell stores: SLC (1 bit), MLC (2 bits), TLC (3 bits), and QLC (4 bits). As NAND manufacturers seek to optimize profitability, they tend to shift capacity from older, smaller nodes running SLC toward advanced nodes producing denser MLC, TLC, and QLC products, creating supply tightness for SLC.

## [RISC-V Is Inevitable, State of the Union Keynote Argues](https://www.eetimes.com/risc-v-is-inevitable-state-of-the-union-keynote-argues/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A State of the Union-style keynote argued that RISC-V's open standard ISA combined with the emergence of RVA23 silicon provides the flexibility and modularity needed for widespread adoption, without the constraints imposed by traditional proprietary systems. If RISC-V's adoption is indeed inevitable, it could fundamentally reshape the semiconductor industry by reducing dependence on proprietary ISAs like Arm and x86, lowering entry barriers for chip designers, and accelerating innovation through an open hardware ecosystem. The RVA23 profile reached version 1.0 in October 2024, and notably makes the V Vector extensions mandatory under RVA23U64, where they were previously optional in RVA22U64. The profile specifies the ISA features available to user-mode execution environments in 64-bit application processors.

rss · EE Times · Jul 13, 12:00

**Background**: RISC-V (pronounced "risk-five") is a free and open standard instruction set architecture (ISA) based on reduced instruction set computer (RISC) principles. Unlike proprietary ISAs such as Arm or x86, RISC-V can be implemented without licensing fees, enabling customization and scalability from small embedded devices to high-performance computing. Architecture profiles like RVA23 define standardized bundles of ISA features for specific application classes, similar to how the industry has historically used profiles to create interoperable subsets across hardware vendors, helping ensure software portability across compliant implementations.
