## [E-means: 10x faster k-means algorithm at equal loss](https://e-means.github.io/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

E-means is a new algorithm claimed to be up to 45x faster than standard k-means at equal loss. It reduces the number of passes over the dataset required for convergence. Significantly accelerating fundamental clustering algorithms directly improves ML pipeline efficiency and reduces training times for data science tasks. This could lead to optimized vector index implementations and faster approximate nearest neighbor searches. Adapting E-means to an IVF index on GPU resulted in a 146x speedup in a specific community project. The method is generally not strictly better in all use cases, such as specific mesh relaxing tasks, but wins by needing fewer data passes.

hackernews · porridgeraisin · Nov 9, 18:45 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50429734)

**Background**: K-means is a standard algorithm used to group a set of objects into a specified number of clusters based on feature similarity. The Lloyd's algorithm is the iterative procedure most commonly used to optimize K-means by minimizing the distance between data points and their cluster centroids.

**Discussion**: The community notes that E-means is highly adaptable, achieving massive GPU speedups when applied to IVF indices. However, there is a debate over its practical advantages in niche tasks, alongside concerns that the hyperparameter scaling laws appear to have been fitted post-hoc.

## [Vector-Based Approach Advances Curvilinear OPC in Semiconductor Manufacturing](https://semiwiki.com/eda/siemens-eda/376032-curvilinear-opc-and-next-gen-photomask-optimization/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Siemens EDA introduced a vector-based approach for Curvilinear Optical Proximity Correction (OPC), which represents a fundamental shift in how photomasks are created and optimized. This new method is designed to address the increased geometric complexity of advanced chip manufacturing by moving away from traditional raster-based techniques. The adoption of Curvilinear OPC is essential for next-generation chip production, as it allows for tighter process requirements and improved manufacturing precision. This shift is critical for the semiconductor industry to continue scaling devices according to advanced technology roadmaps. The core of this new methodology lies in 'vector-based site and anchor decoupling,' which allows for independent fragmentation and control of mask shapes. By decoupling these elements, Siemens EDA's solution enhances the optimization of photomasks for advanced technology nodes.

rss · SemiWiki · Nov 9, 16:00

**Background**: In semiconductor lithography, a photomask acts as a template to pattern layers of a chip, while Optical Proximity Correction (OPC) adjusts these patterns to compensate for optical distortions during exposure. Traditional OPC relied on 'raster' or polygon-based grids, but as nodes shrink, 'curvilinear' shapes are required to maintain high-fidelity image transfer. Vector-based approaches use continuous mathematical curves rather than pixel grids to define these mask shapes.
