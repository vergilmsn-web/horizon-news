## [Postgres LISTEN/NOTIFY Actually Scales Well](https://www.dbos.dev/blog/postgres-listen-notify-scalability) ⭐️ 7.0/10

DBOS published a detailed analysis and benchmarks demonstrating that PostgreSQL's built-in LISTEN/NOTIFY pub/sub mechanism can handle tens of thousands of notifications per second in production workloads. This challenges the common assumption that LISTEN/NOTIFY is only suitable for low-throughput use cases, offering backend engineers a simpler, ACID-consistent alternative to external pub/sub systems like Redis or dedicated message brokers. Real-world patterns include using a small number of LISTEN connections per host (3-4) paired with application-layer subscription fan-out, enabling tens of thousands of end-user subscriptions. Trade-offs include the need for careful queue cleanup with UNLISTEN and the risk of unbounded memory growth from in-session notification queues.

hackernews · KraftyOne · Jul 24, 19:05 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49040296)

**Background**: PostgreSQL's LISTEN and NOTIFY commands implement a lightweight pub/sub system: a session can subscribe to a named channel with LISTEN, and any session can broadcast a notification on that channel with NOTIFY. Each notification can carry a small payload string, and subscribed clients are informed asynchronously. This avoids the operational overhead of running a separate message broker and keeps messaging transactions atomic with regular database writes, which is particularly attractive for workflows that require strong consistency between data changes and event delivery.

## [TSMC CoPoS vs Intel EMIB: Competing Approaches to Chiplet Packaging](https://semiwiki.com/semiconductor-manufacturers/tsmc/371548-tsmc-copos-versus-intel-emib-semiconductor-packaging/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

SemiWiki has published a technical comparison of TSMC's emerging panel-level Chip-on-Panel-on-Substrate (CoPoS) packaging against Intel's established Embedded Multi-die Interconnect Bridge (EMIB) technology, examining how each addresses the challenge of integrating increasingly large heterogeneous chiplet systems. Advanced packaging has become a key battleground as Moore's Law slows, with both TSMC and Intel staking competing claims on how best to assemble multi-chiplet designs used in AI accelerators, high-performance CPUs, and GPUs. The outcome will influence which foundries win leading-edge customer engagements and shape the economics of next-generation semiconductor manufacturing. CoPoS 将传统晶圆级封装流程"面板化"，改用大尺寸矩形面板以提升产能并降低单芯片成本；台积电 CoPoS 试产线已于 2026 年 2 月开始向研发团队交付设备，目标在 2026 年 6 月完成全线建设，并于 2028–2029 年进入量产爬坡。EMIB 则另辟蹊径，在基板内嵌入小型硅桥，通过微凸点实现高密度芯片间互连，无需硅穿孔（TSV）或大型中介层，近期报道显示其良率已接近 90%。

rss · SemiWiki · Jul 24, 17:00

**Background**: Traditional advanced packaging, such as TSMC's widely used CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate), builds redistribution layers and silicon interposers on circular 300 mm wafers, which limits throughput and package size as AI chips demand ever more chiplets. To break this bottleneck, TSMC is moving to rectangular panels that pack far more die per process step. Intel's EMIB instead sidesteps the large interposer problem entirely by placing tiny embedded silicon bridges only where chip-to-chip signals need to cross, keeping the rest of the package on a conventional organic substrate and reducing cost and complexity.
