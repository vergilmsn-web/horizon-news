## [DapuStor J5060 SSD Boosts QLC Writes with Permanent pSLC Cache](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/ssds/dapustor-splits-qlc-ssd-to-create-a-fast-pslc-region-in-dual-mode-drive-trades-6-percent-to-20-percent-of-its-qlc-capacity-for-more-than-7x-faster-random-writes) ⭐️ 7.5/10

DapuStor has introduced the J5060, a 30.72TB QLC SSD that dedicates 800GB of its capacity as a permanent pseudo-SLC (pSLC) region. This dual-mode architecture trades roughly 2.5% of the total capacity to achieve over a 7x improvement in random write performance. This development addresses the significant performance bottleneck of QLC NAND flash, offering a cost-effective middle ground between high-capacity QLC drives and expensive SLC solutions. It provides a viable alternative for high-write-throughput workloads that require both massive storage and high endurance. The drive achieves this by operating a specific portion of the NAND in pSLC mode, where only one bit is stored per cell instead of the four used in standard QLC mode. This dedicated region serves as a high-speed cache, significantly accelerating data transfer rates for random writes.

rss · Tom's Hardware · Sep 22, 10:30

**Background**: NAND flash memory stores data by programming voltage levels in cells, with SLC storing one bit, TLC storing three, and QLC storing four bits per cell for higher density. While QLC offers significantly more storage space at a lower cost, its slower program/erase cycles result in much lower write performance and endurance compared to SLC. Pseudo-SLC (pSLC) mode is a technique where a controller operates multi-level cells (like TLC or QLC) to store only one bit, gaining SLC-like speed and reliability at the cost of a fraction of the total available capacity.
